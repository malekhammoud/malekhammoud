# Stytch vs Kinde: The Ultimate Comparison

> **TL;DR:** Stytch wins for enterprise-grade security and zero-cost entry; Kinde excels for all-in-one SaaS builders needing billing and feature management.

## Deep Dive: Stytch
Stytch delivers a comprehensive identity platform engineered for enterprise-grade security without the enterprise price tag. Its architecture centers on SCIM provisioning, just-in-time user controls, and device-aware MFA, making it ideal for organizations requiring granular access policies and fraud prevention. The platform's pre-built UI components and customizable branding streamline deployment while maintaining strict security postures.

### Standout Features of Stytch
- **Zero-cost entry** with full enterprise authentication suite
- **SCIM provisioning** and just-in-time controls for automated user lifecycle
- **Device-aware MFA** with intelligent rate limiting and bot detection
- **Organization-level policies** including primary/secondary auth controls
- **Built-in multi-tenancy** supporting complex B2B SaaS architectures

## Deep Dive: Kinde
Kinde positions itself as a developer platform that goes beyond authentication to encompass the entire SaaS stack. Its architecture integrates authentication, access management, billing, and feature flags into a unified platform, eliminating the need for multiple vendors. This makes it particularly attractive for startups and indie developers who want to ship quickly without managing separate billing or feature management systems.

### Standout Features of Kinde
- **All-in-one platform** combining auth, billing, and feature management
- **Powerful feature flags** for gradual rollouts and A/B testing
- **Integrated billing management** with subscription handling
- **Page designer** and custom domains for white-label experiences
- **Connected apps** and lead generation capabilities

## The Final Verdict
**Choose Stytch if:**
- You need enterprise-grade security without enterprise pricing
- Your priority is comprehensive authentication and fraud prevention
- You require SCIM provisioning and just-in-time user controls
- Budget constraints make the $0 entry point critical

**Choose Kinde if:**
- You want an all-in-one platform covering auth, billing, and features
- Rapid development and fewer vendors are your top priorities
- You need integrated billing and subscription management
- Feature flags and gradual rollouts are essential to your product strategy
