## Deep Dive: Skype
Skype's architecture centers on VoIP and instant messaging, delivering low-latency, peer-to-peer and server-based communication. Its pay-as-you-go model eliminates subscription overhead, making it ideal for occasional users or businesses needing flexible, on-demand calling. With SMS and limited emergency calling, Skype is optimized for connectivity rather than content depth. Its API supports integrations for messaging and voice, but is not built for content retrieval or analysis.

### Standout Features of Skype
- Pay-as-you-go calling for cost control
- SMS and limited emergency calling capabilities
- Cross-platform real-time messaging and video

## Deep Dive: ProQuest
ProQuest is architected as a massive, centralized research database, aggregating millions of scholarly journals, books, newspapers, and videos. Its platform supports advanced search, saved queries, alerts, and multi-language article translation—features tailored for academic and professional research workflows. ProQuest's API and metadata export tools enable programmatic access, making it a backbone for libraries and institutions requiring deep, structured content discovery.

### Standout Features of ProQuest
- Access to millions of scholarly resources
- Save searches, create alerts, and export citations
- Translation of articles into 20 languages

## The Final Verdict
- **Choose Skype if** you need real-time communication, pay-as-you-go calling, and cross-platform messaging.
- **Choose ProQuest if** you require comprehensive academic research, structured content discovery, and advanced search/alert capabilities.
