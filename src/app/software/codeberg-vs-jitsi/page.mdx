## Deep Dive: Codeberg
Codeberg is a non-profit, community-led platform designed for developers who prioritize privacy and open-source values. Built on Gitea, it offers Git hosting, CI/CD, and Pages with no tracking, no third-party cookies, and optional donations. Its architecture is lightweight, scalable, and optimized for free/libre software projects. Ideal for teams seeking a GitHub alternative without vendor lock-in or surveillance capitalism.

### Standout Features of Codeberg
- **Git Hosting & Pages**: Host repositories and static sites with zero tracking.
- **CI/CD Pipeline**: Automate builds and deployments with integrated workflows.
- **Weblate Integration**: Collaborate on translations directly within projects.

## Deep Dive: Jitsi
Jitsi is a suite of open-source projects focused on secure, scalable video conferencing. At its core, Jitsi Videobridge uses WebRTC to pass video/audio directly between participants, avoiding server-side mixing for better quality and lower latency. Compatible with Ubuntu/Debian packages, it supports simulcast, bandwidth estimation, and scalable video coding. Perfect for teams needing self-hosted, enterprise-grade video solutions.

### Standout Features of Jitsi
- **WebRTC-Native Routing**: Direct peer-to-peer video/audio for minimal latency.
- **Multi-Platform Support**: Android, iOS, and web apps with dial-in/out via Jigasi.
- **Advanced Streaming**: YouTube live streaming and Etherpad integration for collaboration.

## The Final Verdict
**Choose Codeberg if…**
- You need privacy-first Git hosting for open-source projects.
- You want CI/CD and Pages without vendor lock-in.
- You value community-driven, non-profit platforms.

**Choose Jitsi if…**
- You require scalable, self-hosted video conferencing.
- You need WebRTC-native routing for low-latency calls.
- You want advanced features like simulcast and dial-in/out.
